The bog keeps everything.
A shoe. A psalter. Butter
in a firkin, sweet as the day
it went down.

It keeps the weather too —
a wet July from before the famine
pressed flat between the layers
like a flower in a book.

My grandfather cut turf here
with a slean worn thin at the shoulder,
and I have his hands,
and none of his knowledge.

The whole brown patient country
holds its breath beneath me.
I walk out over the centuries
in borrowed boots

and the ground gives
just slightly, everywhere,
the way a thing does
that is still deciding about you.
